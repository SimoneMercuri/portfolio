Tavola SommelierTavola Sommelier

A tool in the Tavola studio for when a wine runs out: the restaurateur decides whether to reorder or replace it, and gets alternatives of the same type and price range, with contacts for the winery and online retailers.

Role
Concept, UX/UI and coded prototype
Project
Personal, an AI concept extending the Tavola studio
Platforms
Tavola studio on web and tablet

Problem

A wine on the list runs out, or drops off the supplier’s price list. The restaurateur has to decide quickly whether to wait and reorder it or replace it with something that fits the list and costs the same. Today they do it from memory, between calls to sales reps and online searches.

Goal

In under a minute: alternatives of the same type and price range, each with an understandable reason, and contacts to order them from the winery or an online retailer. Or the channels to reorder the same wine.

AI design principles

A suggestion is only as good as its explanation.

  1. 01Explain the whyEach alternative says what it shares with the missing wine: grape, area, style, structure.
  2. 02The reference price rulesRanges are worked out from the cost of the bottle being replaced, always visible: no surprises on margin.
  3. 03Say when it doesn’t knowIf nothing in the range is similar enough it says so and offers the nearby range, instead of forcing a result.
  4. 04Lead to the sourceWinery contacts and online retailers with price and availability, to check before ordering.
User flow

From a sold-out wine to an order

  1. StartA wine on the list is sold out
  2. DecisionKeep and reorder it, or replace it?
    • KeepContacts for the winery and retailers that stock it
    • ReplaceAlternatives by type and price, each with its why
  4. ActionContacts the winery or orders from a retailer
  5. EndUpdates the list: the new wine takes the old one’s place

What to look at

  • Keep or replace is the first choice: “Keep it” shows straight away where to reorder.
  • Loading isn’t a spinner: skeleton cards preview the shape of the result.
  • The similarity bar and the “why” make each suggestion checkable.
  • “Not for me” removes a suggestion: the restaurateur corrects the AI without starting over.
  • Minutolo has no cheaper alternatives: the empty state says so and offers the nearby range.

Every state, designed

  • Loading
    Skeleton cards in the shape of the result, announced to screen readers.
  • Results
    Suggestions ranked by similarity, each with its why and contacts.
  • Empty
    No credible alternative: it says so and offers the nearby range.
  • Feedback
    “Not for me” removes the suggestion and updates the message.
  • Limits
    A standing note: check vintage, price and availability before ordering.

What I would measure

  • Time from “sold out” to order sent
  • How often wines are reordered versus replaced, by type
  • Suggestions dismissed with “Not for me”, and why

Next steps

  • Test with 3–5 restaurateurs on a real wine list
  • Connect stock levels: warn before a wine runs out
  • Real winery and distributor price lists, with direct agreements
